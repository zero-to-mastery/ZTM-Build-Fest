# Session script: an older adult using Kin

For epic #603 ("Kin for everyone") and its research story #608. Run it once
before the epic's changes ship (baseline, #610) and once after (follow-up,
#611), with one or two people who rarely use apps (a grandparent is the
example). Kin has no analytics: everything we learn comes from watching and
writing notes, with the person's permission.

## Before the session

- **Who:** someone who uses a phone for calls and messages but rarely
  installs or learns apps. Not a family member who already uses Kin.
- **Device:** their own phone if they're comfortable; otherwise a phone set
  up with large text the way they'd use it.
- **Household:** a test household with a few items, a short chat, and one
  person (the facilitator) as the household admin. Don't use a real family's
  household.
- **Prepare their invitation** the way a family member would (once #612
  ships: "Set up Kin for someone", easy mode on, printed setup sheet).
  Baseline: an ordinary invitation link or QR code.
- **Timer and notes:** a phone timer and the notes template below. No screen
  recording unless the person agrees to it separately, and delete it after
  the notes are written.

## Consent (read aloud, in your own words)

> "We're testing an app for families, not testing you. If something is
> confusing, that's the app's fault, and it's exactly what we want to find.
> I'll ask you to try a few things and think out loud. I won't help unless
> you ask, or unless you've been stuck for a while. I'll write notes but not
> your name, and nothing you type is kept. You can stop at any time."

Ask: "Is it all right if I take notes?" Only continue if they agree.

## Tasks

Give one task at a time, in plain words, without naming buttons. Start the
timer when you finish reading the task; stop it when they finish or give up.
If they're stuck for two minutes, offer a hint and record it.

1. **Open Kin.** "Your family set up an app for you. Here's the sheet/link
   they sent. Please open it and get to the main screen." (Includes choosing
   a password.)
2. **Add something to the shopping list.** "You've run out of milk. Put milk
   on the family shopping list."
3. **Tick something off.** "Someone asked you to water the plants today, and
   you've done it. Show that it's done."
4. **Undo.** "Oops — you didn't actually water them yet. Make it not done
   again."
5. **Read a message.** "Your family sent you a message. Find it and read it."
6. **Reply.** "Send a short reply."
7. **Find help.** "Imagine you don't know what this screen is for. Find out."
8. **Report a problem.** "Imagine something isn't working. Tell the people
   who make the app." (Stop before Send in the baseline if the feature isn't
   there; record what they looked for.)
9. **Come back later.** Close the app. "It's tomorrow. Open the app again and
   check the shopping list."

After the tasks, ask:

- "What was the hardest part?"
- "Was anything on the screen confusing or too small?"
- "Would you use this to keep up with your family? What would stop you?"

## After the session

- File each problem as a GitHub issue under epic #603 (or the release
  checklist), with the task number and what happened. No names, no
  household content.
- In the follow-up, compare: tasks done without help, hints given, time per
  task. The epic's success criterion is tasks 1–6 done without help.
- Delete any recording. Keep only the notes.
