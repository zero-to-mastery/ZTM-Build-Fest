# v0.12.0 — Data Lifecycle, Retention & Deletion

**Status:** Planned. Kin does not yet implement full household deletion or a
service/backup retention policy.

## Prerequisites

- v0.10 local encrypted-storage and archive boundaries remain intact.
- v0.11 durable primary storage, schema migration, backup/restore and operational
  ownership have passed their readiness gate.

## Problem being solved

Kin has domain archival and device/member revocation, but does not yet define
what it promises when a household asks Kin to keep, archive, delete or stop
retaining data. The existing 10,000-event ceiling is a prototype safety bound,
not a retention or growth strategy.

## Scope

Keep these operations and records distinct:

```text
domain archive
member removal
device revocation
local installation reset
household deletion
relay ciphertext deletion
metadata/log retention
backup retention
user-created encrypted archive
event-log compaction
```

Specify an authenticated household-deletion state machine covering destructive
authorization, reachable local encrypted stores and keys, outbox/bindings/cursors,
service envelopes, member/device/session records, provisioning/rotation state,
service metadata, logs, backups, offline devices and user-created archive files.
State what each actor can prove deleted and what remains outside Kin's control.

Define offline-device behavior. A deletion request must leave durable state that
prevents an offline returning installation from silently resurrecting a deleted
household or bypassing revoked authorization. Describe tombstone lifetime,
reconciliation and unreachable-copy limitations.

Set primary-store deletion, backup expiration, restore-after-deletion behavior,
security/operational-log retention and routing-metadata retention rules.
Distinguish deletion from ordinary domain archival and explain any documented
backup expiration window; do not promise instantaneous global erasure.

Evaluate event-log growth options: preserving complete canonical history,
verified encrypted checkpoints, derived snapshots, explicit archive and safe
compaction. Do not compact for convenience. Any optimization must preserve
observable household state while addressing offline devices, tombstones, old
archives, replay compatibility, deletion, conflicts, unknown newer events and
restore.

## Non-goals

- Treating archive, member removal, device revocation or local reset as household
  deletion.
- Claiming Kin erases exported archives, screenshots, remembered information or
  data on unreachable devices.
- Immediate compaction or silent event loss to meet an arbitrary size limit.
- Adding unrelated household/product capabilities.

## Architecture boundaries

Domain event archival remains a replayable household fact. Household deletion is
a separate privacy/destructive service lifecycle. Revocation blocks future access
but cannot recall data already copied. The server continues to hold opaque
ciphertext and limited authorization/routing metadata, not plaintext household
content. Backups, primary storage, logs and client archives have separate
retention owners and guarantees.

## Primary invariants

> Household deletion removes service-controlled primary ciphertext and
> authorization state according to the documented retention policy; exported
> copies and unreachable devices cannot be remotely erased.

Archive Item is not deletion of household history. Revoking a device does not
erase its prior copies. No deleted household is resurrected by a stale device,
backup restore or old relay response. Optimization does not change observable
household state.

## Expected failure scenarios

Test repeated/ambiguous destructive requests, authorization loss mid-delete,
partial local/service deletion, offline devices returning after deletion,
primary deletion followed by stale backup restore, expired and unexpired backups,
log/metadata retention expiry, restore with a tombstone, quota/transaction
failure, unknown future events, concurrent old-device writes and compaction
rollback.

## Acceptance/readiness criteria

- The lifecycle terms above have separate definitions and effects; no UI/API
  operation conflates them.
- A state machine specifies request, authorization, tombstoning, primary deletion,
  backup expiration and terminal outcomes.
- Offline-device and restore tests prove deleted households cannot silently
  return or bypass authorization.
- The retention schedule names each service-controlled data class, owner,
  deletion/expiration rule and restore consequence.
- User-facing promises explicitly exclude exported files, unreachable devices
  and other copies Kin cannot control.
- Any checkpoint/compaction proposal has proof/tests for full state equivalence,
  offline-device reconciliation, replay compatibility, unknown-event preservation,
  rollback and deletion semantics; otherwise history is retained.
- No retention or deletion guarantee is stated beyond verified implementation.

## Deferred decisions

Exact backup window, log/metadata periods, deletion propagation protocol and
whether/when verified checkpoints or compaction are justified are future
implementation decisions. Preserve canonical history until a separately
reviewed design passes the stated invariants.

## Relationship to next release

v0.12 defines which surviving stores and capabilities exist after deletion,
retention expiry and restore. v0.13 uses those facts to define household
continuity and recovery outcomes when credentials, devices or server data are
lost.

## Proposed cadence

```text
v0.12.0 — Household Deletion Lifecycle
v0.12.1 — Multi-Device Deletion Correctness
v0.12.2 — Retention / Checkpoint Resilience
v0.12.3 — Privacy & Data-Lifecycle Hardening
```

These are planning candidates, not promises to manufacture four patches.
