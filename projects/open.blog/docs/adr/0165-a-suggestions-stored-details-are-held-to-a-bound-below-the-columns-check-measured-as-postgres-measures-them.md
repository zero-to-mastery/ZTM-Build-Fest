# ADR 0165: A suggestion's stored details are held to a bound below the column's check, measured as Postgres measures them

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-26
- Decision issue: [#227](https://github.com/journey-to-code-org/open.blog/issues/227)
- Parent story: [#204](https://github.com/journey-to-code-org/open.blog/issues/204)
- Parent epic: [#16](https://github.com/journey-to-code-org/open.blog/issues/16)
- Builds on: [ADR 0153](0153-a-suggestion-is-untrusted-reader-text-with-a-safe-public-link-and-bibliographic-hints-never-fetched.md), [ADR 0154](0154-a-suggestion-is-one-feedback-row-with-no-passage-target-and-one-loosened-check.md)

## Context

The submission is bounded at 12 KiB as sent, and `details` has a check of `octet_length(details::text)
<= 16384`. A link is stored percent-encoded and re-written, so a CJK address of three bytes a
character is nine bytes stored, and Postgres writes jsonb text with a space after every `:` and `,`.
A suggestion that passed the 12 KiB check could therefore exceed 16 KiB when stored and fail its
insert, every time, with a 502.

## Decision

- **A submission whose stored `details` would exceed 11 KiB is refused with `tooLarge`**, by both
  `Submission::from_json` and the browser's `readSuggestionSubmissionJson`, at the same point, so
  the form says it is too long and the function stores nothing. The size is measured as Postgres
  measures `details::text`: the compact JSON plus one byte for every `:` and `,` outside a string,
  of exactly `{type, url?, hints?, about?, contact?}`. 11 KiB leaves the room eight links need at
  their widest ([ADR 0162](0162-a-link-to-future-work-is-kept-in-the-suggestions-details-with-an-audit-event-and-needs-no-new-schema.md)),
  and a test sums them.
- **A shared fixture case** (links of three-byte characters that percent-encoding makes too large
  for the stored row) holds both sides to it; `tests/fixtures/future-suggestion-submissions.json`
  is regenerated by the Rust side.
- **The whole stored record is read under 32 KiB, not 16 KiB.** A record is the text (the row's
  `body`, at most 8,000 bytes), `details` (under its own 16 KiB check) and the name, receipt and
  revision (about 1.5 KiB), so a text of four-byte characters with links at the details bound made
  a record of 18.5 KiB that was stored and then refused on reading. The columns already bound each
  part; `MAX_JSON_BYTES` is now their sum with room to spare, one limit, so every accepted
  submission still reads back after eight links and a clarification. A shared fixture case and a
  test with eight widest links and a clarification hold it.
