## teaching: Teaching

Explain something so that someone new can follow.

### explain-to-a-beginner: Explain it to a beginner
words: 80-200
Pick something you know well and explain it to a friend who has never heard of it. Start from where they are, not from where you are.

### a-tutorial-opening: Open a tutorial
words: 60-150
Write the first few paragraphs of a how-to for something you do often. Say who it is for and what they will be able to do by the end.

### a-concept-without-code: A concept without jargon
words: 80-200
Take one idea from your work or hobby and explain it with no technical words at all, only an everyday comparison.

### the-step-everyone-skips: The step everyone skips
words: 60-150
Describe a step in a process that beginners skip, why it matters, and what happens when they do.

### what-i-wish-i-had-known: What I wish I had known
words: 80-200
Tell someone starting out the one thing you wish somebody had told you on day one.

## stories: Stories

Tell it as a story, the way you would at a table.

### a-mistake-i-made: A mistake I made
words: 80-200
Tell the story of a mistake you made. What happened, how did it feel in the moment, and what did you do next?

### a-day-in-the-life: A day in the life
words: 80-200
Walk through an ordinary day of yours from waking up to going to bed, including the boring parts.

### a-turning-point: A turning point
words: 80-200
Describe a moment when something changed for you, a decision or a surprise, and what your life looked like just before it.

### a-place-you-know-well: A place you know well
words: 80-200
Describe a place you know so well you could walk it with your eyes shut. What do you see, hear and smell?

### the-first-time: The first time
words: 80-200
Tell about the first time you did something that is now routine for you.

## opinions: Opinions

Say what you actually think.

### disagree-with-something: Disagree with something
words: 80-200
Name something most people in your field or circle accept, say why you disagree, and be fair to the other side.

### a-rant-then-a-fix: A rant, then a fix
words: 80-200
Rant for a minute about something that annoys you. Then spend the rest of your time on what you would do about it.

### i-changed-my-mind: I changed my mind
words: 80-200
Write about something you used to believe and no longer do. What changed your mind?

### an-unpopular-preference: An unpopular preference
words: 60-150
Describe something you like that most people do not, and defend it without apologizing.

## reviews: Reviews

Judge something you have actually used.

### a-tool-you-use-daily: A tool you use every day
words: 80-200
Review a tool, app or gadget you use every day. What do you love, what grates, and who should buy it?

### a-book-film-or-album: A book, film or album
words: 80-200
Tell a friend about a book, film or album you keep coming back to, without spoilers and without the plot summary.

### a-purchase-you-regret: A purchase you regret
words: 60-150
Describe something you bought that did not live up to it. What did you expect and what did you get?

### the-best-thing-you-bought: The best thing you ever bought
words: 60-150
Describe the purchase that paid you back the most, in money or in daily happiness.

## people: People

Write about the people who shaped you.

### praise-someone: Praise someone
words: 80-200
Write a thank-you to a teammate, teacher or friend, with one specific thing they did that you still remember.

### advice-someone-gave-you: Advice someone gave you
words: 60-150
Quote the best advice anyone gave you. Who said it, when, and did you take it right away?

### a-letter-to-your-younger-self: A letter to your younger self
words: 80-200
Write to yourself at eighteen. What do you want to say, and what would you leave for them to find out?

### someone-who-changed-how-you-work: Someone who changed how you work
words: 80-200
Describe a person whose habits rubbed off on you. What did you copy from them?

## craft: Craft

How you think about doing good work.

### how-i-think-about: How I think about it
words: 80-200
Pick one part of your work and describe how you think about it, as if explaining your rule of thumb to an apprentice.

### how-i-know-when-to-stop: How I know when to stop
words: 60-150
Describe how you decide a piece of work is finished. What are the signs?

### what-good-enough-means: What good enough means to me
words: 60-150
Say what "good enough" looks like for you, and where you refuse to settle.

### my-workspace-and-habits: My workspace and habits
words: 60-150
Describe where and how you do your best work: the place, the time of day, the things on the desk.

## humor: Humor

Be funny on purpose, or by accident.

### complain-about-something-mundane: Complain about something mundane
words: 60-150
Complain, with total commitment, about something tiny: a pen that skips, a lid that never fits, a queue that never moves.

### an-absurd-how-to: An absurd how-to
words: 60-150
Write serious step-by-step instructions for something that needs none, or something that cannot be done.

### the-worst-advice-that-worked: The worst advice that worked
words: 60-150
Tell about advice that sounded terrible and somehow turned out right, or the reverse.

### a-minor-disaster: A minor disaster
words: 80-200
Tell about a small disaster that is funny now. Do not skip the part where you were certain it would be fine.

## expertise: Expertise

Share what you know that others do not.

### something-most-people-do-not-know: Something most people do not know
words: 80-200
Tell us something you know from your job or hobby that surprises people when they hear it.

### a-question-you-get-asked-often: A question you are asked often
words: 80-200
Take a question people keep asking you and answer it properly, the way you wish you always had time to.

### a-myth-in-your-field: A myth in your field
words: 80-200
Name a common belief in your field that is wrong or only half true, and explain what is actually going on.

### how-to-tell-good-from-bad: How to tell good from bad
words: 60-150
Describe how you can tell quality work from poor work in your field, in the first minute, before an expert would be needed.

## everyday-life: Everyday life

The ordinary things that make you who you are.

### your-town: Your town
words: 80-200
Describe your town or neighborhood to someone who has never been. Where do you go, and what would you show a visitor?

### a-meal-without-a-recipe: A meal you make without a recipe
words: 60-150
Tell how you make a dish you have never measured. Include the shortcuts and the mistakes you have made.

### a-routine-you-keep: A routine you keep
words: 60-150
Describe a small routine you keep, a morning habit or a weekly ritual, and what would be lost if you stopped.

### something-on-your-shelf: Something on your shelf
words: 60-150
Pick an object on a shelf near you and tell its story: where it came from and why you still have it.

## openings-and-endings: Openings and endings

Practice the first and last words of a piece.

### a-post-ending: A post ending
words: 40-120
Imagine a post you have not written. Write its last paragraph so that a reader closes the page feeling something.

### three-first-paragraphs: Three first paragraphs
words: 90-210
Write three first paragraphs for three different posts you might write, one for each, in three different moods.

### a-title-and-a-first-line: A title and a first line
words: 40-120
Write five possible titles for something you want to write, then the first line that follows your favorite.

### a-goodbye: A goodbye
words: 60-150
Say goodbye to something: a place, a job, a habit or a season. Keep it short and true.

## trades: Trades

Talk about the work, the customers and the days, the way you would on a job site.

### a-job-that-went-sideways: A job that went sideways
words: 80-200
Tell about a job that did not go the way you planned. What went wrong, what did you do about it, and how did it end?

### the-question-every-customer-asks: The question every customer asks
words: 60-150
What is the one question customers ask you again and again? Answer it the way you would out loud, standing in their kitchen.

### the-tool-i-would-never-give-up: The tool I would never give up
words: 60-150
Pick the one tool you would fight to keep. Say what it does, how you came to own it, and what you would do without it.

### a-myth-about-my-trade: A myth about my trade
words: 60-150
What do people believe about your trade that is not true? Say what they think and what is really going on.

### how-i-price-a-job-honestly: How I price a job honestly
words: 80-200
Walk through how you work out what a job costs and what you tell the customer. Say what you do when it will cost more than they hoped.

### the-best-customer-i-ever-had: The best customer I ever had
words: 80-200
Tell about a customer you were glad to work for. What did they do that made it easy, and what stuck with you afterward?

### what-i-check-before-i-start: What I check before I start
words: 60-150
Before you touch anything, what do you look at? Tell it in the order you do it, and say what each check saves you from.

### the-day-i-knew-i-was-good-at-this: The day I knew I was good at this
words: 80-200
Think of the moment you realized you had it figured out. Where were you, what had just happened, and what did it feel like?
